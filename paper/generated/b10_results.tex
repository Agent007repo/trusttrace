\textbf{Results.} The author labelled 17 atomic obligations across the ten texts. He drafted them first and then entered them in the labelling tool; the entries span 3 minutes, which is entry time, not total labelling effort. Table~\ref{tab:b10} gives per-arm results. The guided minus naive difference in macro F1, with 95\% bootstrap intervals over texts, was Qwen2.5-1.5B: -0.010 [-0.121, +0.090], guided better on 3, naive better on 3, tied on 4 of 10 texts; Mistral-7B: +0.050 [+0.000, +0.117], guided better on 2, naive better on 0, tied on 8 of 10 texts. The guided prompt lowered the share of predictions whose evidence span was copied exactly from the source (Qwen2.5-1.5B 0.93 naive vs 0.81 guided; Mistral-7B 0.94 naive vs 0.63 guided), so any recall gain came with weaker grounding. On the 30 audited pairs the matcher agreed with the author's blinded judgement on 87\% of items (Cohen's $\kappa=0.73$). 100\% of the pairs it accepted were judged the same obligation (95\% Wilson interval 80 to 100\%), and 27\% of the near-misses it rejected were judged the same obligation (11 to 52\%). Because the sample was stratified 15/15, these figures describe the audited pairs, not matcher accuracy over all pairs. By the rule fixed before collection ($\kappa\geq0.6$), F1 values are treated as reliable for this pilot. Median generation time for the guided arm was Qwen2.5-1.5B 13\,s (CPU, 2 threads, cloud container), Mistral-7B 5\,s (Metal GPU, M1 Max).%
