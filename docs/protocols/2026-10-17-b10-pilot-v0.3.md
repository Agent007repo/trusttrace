# B10 feasibility pilot, protocol v0.3 (17 October 2026)

Identity: `TrustTrace/B10-pilot/2026-10-17/v0.3`. Frozen and committed before any pilot model output was generated or seen. The machine-readable version is [`configs/b10_pilot.json`](../../configs/b10_pilot.json). The immutable 13 October preregistration is unchanged. This appendix defines a smaller study that runs under it as a pilot, and every difference is logged in `DEVIATIONS.md`.

## Why a pilot

The full B10 design needs 24 texts (12 of them verified legal excerpts), two model families, five seeds, three conditions and human one-to-one adjudication of every prediction. That is 720 generations and several thousand matching decisions. Two inputs are not ready: the legal excerpts still lack verified operative text and reuse terms, and there is no independent retrieval corpus. The application timeline also limits human time.

A pilot answers the questions a full study needs answered first. Can the labelling scheme be applied consistently? Do small local models return valid, grounded output? How large is the guided versus naive difference likely to be? Can matching be automated reliably enough to cut adjudication cost? The pilot does not confirm or reject H1.

## Design

| Element | Full study (13 Oct) | Pilot (this appendix) |
|---|---|---|
| Texts | 24 (12 synthetic, 12 legal) | 10 synthetic, drawn with `random.Random(20261013)` |
| Gold labels | Samarth, full set | Samarth, all 10 pilot texts |
| Models | two families | Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct and Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.3, both Q4_K_M GGUF |
| Conditions | naive, guided, guided-retrieval | naive, guided (retrieval deferred) |
| Seeds | five | three |
| Generations | 720 | 120 |
| Matching | human, blinded, every prediction | pre-registered automatic matcher, validated by a blinded human audit of 30 pairs |
| Runtime | Samarth's Mac (pending) | llama-cpp-python on a 2-vCPU Linux cloud container |

Prompts are the 14 October drafts, unchanged, plus a fixed output-contract line that both arms receive. Decoding settings are the 14 October proposal: temperature 0.2, top-p 0.95, 1024 new tokens.

## Matching rule, fixed before outputs exist

For every predicted and gold requirement in the same text, the pair score is `0.6 * cosine(statement embeddings) + 0.4 * token F1 of the two evidence spans`. Embeddings come from `BAAI/bge-small-en-v1.5`. Pairs scoring at least 0.6 are eligible. The final match is the one-to-one assignment with the highest total score. The weights and threshold are assumptions, not tuned values.

To test whether this rule agrees with human judgement, Samarth audits 30 pairs: 15 the algorithm matched and 15 near-misses it rejected, shuffled, with model, condition, seed and the algorithm's decision hidden. We report percent agreement and Cohen's kappa. If kappa is below 0.6, the F1 results are reported as unreliable and we make no directional claim about H1.

## Sealing

Model generation may begin before the human labels are locked, which saves a day. Raw outputs stay off any branch Samarth reads until the labels are locked. A checksum manifest of every raw output is committed first, so the order of events can be checked afterwards.

## Analysis

Per-run requirement F1, per-text means over seeds, and macro F1 per model and condition. The guided minus naive contrast is paired by text, with 2000 bootstrap resamples over texts (seed 20261013) and a 95 percent percentile interval. Slot agreement is exploratory. Feasibility outcomes are reported alongside: valid JSON rate, exact evidence rate, latency, labelling time and matcher agreement.

## What the pilot cannot show

It cannot show that guidance helps in general, because 10 short synthetic texts are not a population. It cannot show anything about legal text. It cannot show a retrieval effect. The Mistral file is a third-party conversion whose source revision we did not verify. A single annotator means no inter-annotator agreement. The full study remains specified in the 13 October preregistration and the 14 October appendix.
